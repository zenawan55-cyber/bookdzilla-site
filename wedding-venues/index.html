Where venue bookings get lost.

Most venues don't have a lead problem. They have a reply problem. Couples plan in the evenings and on weekends, the same hours your team is running events or off the clock. They send the same enquiry to several venues at once, then tour the ones that answer with a date and a price.

Speed matters more than it feels like it should. In a study of 1.25 million online leads at 42 US companies, firms that responded within an hour were about seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that responded even one hour later, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited a day or longer. That research wasn't about weddings, but anyone who has watched a couple book the venue that called back first knows it applies.

The losses happen in four places:

  • The first reply comes too late. A Thursday-night enquiry answered on Monday morning is usually already touring somewhere else.
  • The reply has no answer in it. "Thanks for reaching out, when can you hop on a call?" makes the couple do more work. They want to know if the date is open and roughly what it costs.
  • Nobody follows up. Couples go quiet because they are busy, not because they said no. One reply and silence loses people who were still deciding.
  • Phone calls go to voicemail. Most callers don't leave a message. They call the next venue on the list.

Engagement season is when this costs the most.

About 40% of engagements happen between November and February, and December is the single biggest month. Newly engaged couples start shopping for venues within weeks, so January and February are often the busiest enquiry months of the year for venues.

That is also when a slow reply costs the most. With an average venue spend of $12,900, losing even two tours a month to a faster competitor adds up quickly. The best time to fix your reply process is before the rush, not in the middle of it.

What you can set up with today's tools.

None of this needs a new website or a new booking platform. It connects to the inbox, phone number and calendar you already use.

  1. Answer every enquiry in under a minute

    Route every source into one place: your website form, The Knot and WeddingWire messages, Instagram and Facebook DMs, Google Business messages and email. Each new enquiry gets a written reply within a minute, any time of day. Assistants built on models like Claude or ChatGPT can write that reply from your real prices, packages and policies, in your tone, and hand the conversation to you the moment someone asks for a person.

  2. Lead with the date and a price range

    Check the requested date against your calendar and say whether it's open. Then give the starting price for that day of the week and guest count. You don't have to publish your full price sheet. "Saturdays for up to 150 start at $6,500" is enough to keep the conversation going.

  3. Offer two tour times, not a link to figure out

    Ask "Thursday at 4pm or Saturday at 11am?" instead of "let us know when works." Two concrete options get a yes far more often than an open question. When they pick one, it goes straight onto your calendar through Calendly, Cal.com, GoHighLevel or the system you use now. Offer a video tour for out-of-town couples.

  4. Remind them so they actually show up

    Send a confirmation right away, a reminder the day before and a short text two hours before with parking and who to ask for. If someone misses a tour, send a rebooking message the same day.

  5. Follow up until they decide

    Couples who go quiet get short, personal follow-ups: day 1 (a photo of the space set for a wedding their size), day 3 (answers to the question most couples ask next), day 7 (whether their date is still open) and day 14 (a friendly last check-in). Stop the moment they reply.

  6. Text back every missed call

    When a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text within seconds: sorry we missed you, is this about a date, here are two times to talk. Most people who would have dialed the next venue reply to the text instead.

  7. Follow up after the tour

    The tour isn't the booking. Send the proposal the same day, a check-in two days later and a reminder before any hold on their date expires. This is where most venues leave money on the table.

  8. Bring back old enquiries

    Your inbox is full of couples who asked about a date last year and never booked. A short message to the ones whose dates are still open, or who never chose a venue, turns some of them into tours without any ad spend.

  9. Ask for reviews at the right moment

    Send a review request for Google and The Knot a few days after the wedding, while the couple is still happy and sharing photos. More recent reviews means more couples contacting you in the first place.

What a good first reply looks like.

Here is a reply that answers the question, gives a reason to keep talking and moves straight to a tour. Copy the shape, not the words.

Hi Maya, congratulations! June 13 is open right now. Saturdays for up to 150 guests start at $6,500 and include tables, chairs, the bridal suite and the garden ceremony site.

The easiest way to see if it feels right is a tour. I have Thursday at 4pm or Saturday at 11am. Which works better?

And here is the reply most venues send:

Thank you for your interest! Please find our brochure attached. Let us know a good time for a call.

The second one isn't rude. It just answers nothing and asks the couple to do the next step, at 9pm, while three other venues are also in their inbox.

Test your own venue in five minutes.

Before you change anything, measure where you are. We run this same test before every call.

  1. On a Thursday or Friday evening, send an enquiry through your own website form from a personal email. Ask about a Saturday next year, 120 guests and pricing.
  2. Write down the exact time you sent it.
  3. Note when the first reply arrives, and whether it answers the date and the price.
  4. Repeat it with a call at lunchtime and a DM on Instagram.

If the reply took more than an hour, or didn't answer the question, you have found where tours are going.
