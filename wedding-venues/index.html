Where venue tours leak.

Most venues don’t have a lead problem. They pay for ads and listings, the enquiries come in, and then couples drift away before the tour. Couples plan in the evenings and on weekends, send the same enquiry to several venues at once, and tour the ones that answer with a date and a price.

Speed matters more than it feels like it should. In a study of 1.25 million online leads at 42 US companies, firms that responded within an hour were about seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that responded even one hour later, and more than 60 times as likely as firms that waited a day or longer.

The losses happen in five places:

  • The ad and the page say different things. The ad shows the ceremony lawn at sunset; the page opens on a generic “Welcome to our venue”.
  • Price and dates are nowhere. Couples rank price first. A page with no starting price makes them ask, and many won’t.
  • Nothing for couples not ready to tour. Many are just engaged and still comparing. With nothing to take, they leave.
  • The first reply comes too late. A Thursday-night enquiry answered on Monday is usually already touring somewhere else.
  • Nobody follows up. Couples go quiet because they’re busy, not because they said no.

Engagement season is when this costs the most.

About 40% of engagements happen between November and February, and December is the single biggest month. Newly engaged couples start shopping for venues within weeks, so January and February are often the busiest enquiry months of the year.

That is also when a slow reply costs the most. With an average venue spend of $12,900, losing even two tours a month to a faster venue adds up quickly. The best time to fix the path is before the rush.

A page that keeps the ad’s promise.

  1. Repeat the promise

    If the ad sells “Fall Saturdays from $6,500”, the page leads with exactly that. Couples who clicked for it should see it in the first second.

  2. Show the starting price and open dates

    “Saturdays for up to 150 guests from $6,500” and a short list of open dates this season. You don’t have to publish your full price sheet.

  3. Put your rating on the first screen

    Your Google or The Knot rating with the review count, plus one review that mentions the day going smoothly.

  4. Make the tour one tap away

    Two tour times or a short form visible without scrolling on a phone, and a video tour option for out-of-town couples.

A free offer for couples not ready to tour.

Many couples who click aren’t ready to book a tour yet. Offer an open-dates and pricing check: they enter a month and a guest count, and get back the open Saturdays and the starting price for each. It’s useful to them, and it gives you a name and an email weeks before they decide.

What a good first reply looks like.

Here is a reply that answers the question, gives a reason to keep talking and moves straight to a tour. Copy the shape, not the words.

Hi Maya, congratulations! June 12 is open right now. Saturdays for up to 150 guests start at $6,500 and include tables, chairs, the bridal suite and the garden ceremony site.

The easiest way to see if it feels right is a tour. I have Thursday at 4pm or Saturday at 11am. Which works better?

And here is the reply most venues send:

Thank you for your interest! Please find our brochure attached. Let us know a good time for a call.

The second one isn’t rude. It just answers nothing and asks the couple to do the next step, at 9pm, while three other venues are also in their inbox.

Follow-up until they decide.

Couples who go quiet get short, personal follow-ups: day 1 (a photo of the space set for a wedding their size), day 3 (answers to the question most couples ask next), day 7 (whether their date is still open) and day 14 (a friendly last check-in). Stop the moment they reply.

The tour isn’t the booking. Send the proposal the same day, check in two days later and remind them before any hold on their date expires. Missed calls get a text back within seconds, and a missed tour gets a rebooking message the same day.

Test your own venue in five minutes.

  1. Find your live ads in the Meta Ad Library and the Google Ads Transparency Center, and tap through each one on your phone. Does the page repeat the promise, show a starting price and your rating?
  2. On a Thursday or Friday evening, send an enquiry through your own website form from a personal email. Ask about a Saturday next year, 120 guests and pricing.
  3. Write down when the first reply arrives, and whether it answers the date and the price.
  4. Repeat it with a call at lunchtime and a DM on Instagram.

If the reply took more than an hour, or didn’t answer the question, you have found where tours are going. We run this same test before every first email.
