Where new patients get lost.

Most practices spend money to make the phone ring, then lose a share of those patients in the gap between the enquiry and the first real answer. It's rarely the front desk's fault. They are checking patients in, verifying insurance and answering the phone at the same time.

Speed matters more than it feels like it should. In a study of 1.25 million online leads at 42 US companies, firms that responded within an hour were about seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that responded even one hour later. A new patient who filled in your form at night and hears back mid-morning has often booked with the practice that answered first.

The losses happen in four places:

  • Calls during the busy hours. Lunch, the first hour of the day and after 5pm are when patients call and when the desk is busiest. Most callers who reach voicemail don't leave a message.
  • Forms and messages answered the next day. Website forms, Google Business messages and Facebook or Instagram DMs often wait until someone has time.
  • The insurance question. "Do you take my insurance?" is the most common first question, and a slow or vague answer sends people back to their insurer's directory.
  • Patients who drift away. Overdue recalls and unscheduled treatment plans quietly become someone else's patients.

What you can set up with today's tools.

None of this replaces your practice management software. It sits in front of it, on your existing phone number, forms and inbox.

  1. Text back every missed call

    When a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text within seconds from your practice number asking how you can help. Most people who would have called the next practice reply to the text, and the conversation continues there.

  2. Answer every form and message in under a minute

    Route your website form, Google Business messages and social DMs into one inbox. Each new patient gets a reply within a minute, written from your real services, insurance list, new patient offer and hours. Assistants built on models like Claude or ChatGPT can write these in your tone and hand off to your team the moment a question needs a person.

  3. Answer insurance and price first

    Say whether you're in network with their plan, or what the new patient exam costs without insurance. Then offer two appointment times. Clinical questions always go to your team, never to the assistant.

  4. Book into your schedule

    Depending on your setup, the patient picks a time that goes straight onto the calendar, or the request goes to the front desk to confirm in Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental or whatever you use. Either way the patient gets an answer now and a confirmation later.

  5. Confirm and remind to cut no-shows

    Send a confirmation when they book, a reminder two days before with new patient forms and what to bring, and a text the morning of. Anyone who misses a visit gets a rebooking message the same day.

  6. Run recall and reactivation

    Patients overdue for a cleaning, and patients with treatment plans they never scheduled, get a short, personal message with two times. These are the cheapest appointments you will ever fill, because those patients already know you.

  7. Ask for reviews after good visits

    A review request a few hours after the appointment, while the visit is fresh, keeps your Google Business Profile growing. More recent reviews means more calls from people searching "dentist near me".

A note on patient privacy.

Messages from patients can include health information, so the tools matter. We build on platforms that offer HIPAA options and sign a Business Associate Agreement where the platform provides one, keep messages to booking and logistics, and route anything clinical to your team. Your patient records stay in your practice management software.

What a good first reply looks like.

Here is a reply to a new patient form that answers the question and moves straight to a booking. Copy the shape, not the words.

Hi Jordan, thanks for reaching out to Bright Smile Dental. Yes, we're in network with Delta Dental PPO, and a new patient exam, X-rays and cleaning is usually covered.

I have Thursday at 8am or Friday at 3:30pm. Which works better for you?

And here is the reply many practices send the next morning:

Thank you for contacting our office. Please call us during business hours to schedule.

The second one asks the patient to do the work again, during the same hours they couldn't call the first time.

Test your own practice in five minutes.

Before you change anything, measure where you are.

  1. Call your own practice from a personal phone at 12:30pm and again at 5:45pm. Note whether a person answers.
  2. On a Sunday evening, fill in your website form as a new patient asking whether you take a common insurance plan.
  3. Send a message through your Google Business Profile.
  4. Write down when each one gets a real answer, and whether it offered a time.

If any of them took more than an hour, that is where new patients are going.
